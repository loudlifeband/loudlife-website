# My Guitar is Janky, But It's Got Heart

Some guitars cost thousands. Mine cost me a story.

I found it at a pawn shop off Sunset — the kind of place where nothing has a price tag and everything smells like cigarette smoke and broken dreams. It was buried behind a stack of dusty amps, neck slightly warped, one tuning peg held on by hope and electrical tape.

The guy behind the counter said it had been there for years. Nobody wanted it. I said I'd take it.

## The Sound

Here's the thing about a janky guitar: it doesn't play clean. The action is too high, the intonation is off, and the pickups buzz like a swarm of electric bees. But that's the point. That buzz, that rattle, that fight between your fingers and the fretboard — that's where the sound lives.

Perfect guitars make perfect music. But we don't play perfect music. We play **loud** music. We play music that bleeds.

## Why It Matters

Every dent on this guitar is a show. Every scratch is a story. The cigarette burn on the headstock? That's from our first gig at The Roxy when the stage was too small and the amps were too loud and everything was exactly right.

You don't need a $3,000 Les Paul to make something real. You need something that fights back. Something that makes you work for every note.

## The Point

Your gear doesn't define your sound. **You** define your sound. Play what you've got. Play it loud. Play it like you mean it.

That's what Loud Life is about.
